# Vetting sources: the three-check rule

Brand names mean little; credible reviews mean a lot. Small internet-native brands are
fine and often where the value is.

Nothing gets recommended without all three:
1. **A hands-on review**: someone who bought, used or tested it.
2. **Real-user opinions**: posts from people with the same use (same machine, same craft,
   same size).
3. **A live store check**: price, stock and returns seen in the browser today.

## How much to trust a source
- **Strong:** hands-on testers such as Wirecutter, the Strategist, and specialist blogs with
  their own photos and measurements.
- **Medium:** curated roundups that are lighter on testing.
- **Signal only, never the sole reason:** SEO listicles, brand blogs, affiliate comparison
  sites, YouTube reviews with discount codes (paid to like it).
- **Community:** Reddit and hobby forums. Give more weight to posters with the same use.

## Rules
- A claim from a single source gets labeled unverified.
- Amazon: check price history (camelcamelcamel.com or keepa.com) before calling it a deal.
  Star counts can be gamed; the 3-star reviews show the real failure modes.
- Stop at 2–4 checked options. That beats 10 unchecked ones.
- "Nothing meets the bar at this budget" is a valid result.
- **Membership stores (Costco, Sam's):** prices are often hidden behind login. Say where
  the price came from and that it's unconfirmed.
- **Same item, different names:** a product can be sold under different names on different
  sites. Match the manufacturer's model number before comparing prices.
- **Discontinued favorites:** if their known-good item is gone, say so and offer the closest
  checked replacement. Never swap silently on a repeat order.
