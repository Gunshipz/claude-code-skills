# Sourcing & Ordering

Find, vet and line up a purchase. The pipeline is the same for every store; what you
learn about specific stores goes in a store-notes file so the next order is faster.

**Store notes:** keep `Store Notes.md` in the user's notes (their second brain or
Documents). Read it before opening any store; add what you learned after each session
(checkout quirks, where the codes come from, which listing was the real one).

## Hard rules

1. **Never enter payment details and never place the order.** Drive checkout to the
   payment step, then hand off with the exact total and the clicks left.
2. **Passwords and logins are the user's.** If a store needs an account, the user signs
   in or creates it in their own browser. Never type or store their password.
3. **Never make up personal details** on forms. Sizes, addresses and preferences come from
   the user's notes, or you ask once and save the answer.
4. **No recommendation without three checks** (references/vetting-sources.md): a hands-on
   review, real-user opinions, and a live look at the store. "Nothing good at this budget"
   is a valid answer.
5. **Check it live before recommending:** price, stock in the size or color they need,
   shipping cost and return policy. Search snippets go stale; carts don't.

## Pipeline

1. **What do they need?** Look things up before asking: their notes, past orders in their
   email if connected, earlier store notes. Ask only what can't be looked up: budget,
   quantity, must-haves. For packs and consumables, confirm the total quantity, not just
   the item. Ask what it's for, because the use decides how good it needs to be.
2. **Find candidates** per references/vetting-sources.md. Bring back 2–4 options, each with
   the reason it's credible, not a top-10 list.
3. **Check them live** per references/browser-tactics.md: exact listing link, price,
   availability in their size or color (the add-to-cart test), shipping and returns. Check
   the brand's own store as well as marketplaces; stock and prices differ. On Shopify
   stores, the product's `.js` endpoint gives the true price and stock (see browser-tactics).
4. **Discount pass** per references/discount-hunting.md. Report list price → code → net,
   plus any cashback. A code counts only once the cart total actually drops.
5. **Check every change you make on a site** by reloading and reading it back. Never trust
   the button you clicked.
6. **Checkout up to the line.** Fill shipping and codes, stop at payment or at a sign-in
   wall for an account they own. Hand off with the total, what's left to click, the
   delivery window and the return terms. Then log the order in their notes and update
   Store Notes.

## Craft supplies and blanks

- **Sublimation blanks** need a polyester coating or high-polyester fabric (light colors
  print best). Check the listing for "sublimation" specifically, not just "blank".
- **HTV and adhesive vinyl** are different products. Match the vinyl type to the job
  (iron-on vs. permanent vs. removable) and the cutter's supported materials.
- **Bulk vs. small packs:** compare per-unit price including shipping. Craft-store coupons
  (weekly app or email coupons) often beat marketplace prices on single items.
- Check what they already own (their notes) before buying a duplicate.
